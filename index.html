Motivation

Why Flow Policies for Robot Control?

Likelihood-based policy gradient methods like PPO have enabled impressive robot control results across legged locomotion, humanoid control, and manipulation. However, these methods require differentiable action likelihoods, which constrains policy outputs to simple distributions such as diagonal Gaussians. This limitation is problematic for complex robotic tasks that may benefit from more expressive action distributions.

Flow-based generative models have emerged as the frontier for supervised policy learning in robotics. Policies like Diffusion Policy and π0 have demonstrated success in learning manipulation from human demonstrations, including at large scale with language conditioning. However, these approaches rely on offline behavior cloning and cannot be improved through online interaction with the environment.

Key Insight: Flow policies can express more complex, multi-modal action distributions than Gaussian policies. This enables better exploration during training and the ability to capture coordinated, symmetric behaviors (e.g., alternating gaits in locomotion) that are difficult to represent with independent per-dimension sampling.

Reinforcement learning algorithms that support flow-based representations promise to:

  • Enable more expressive exploration: Flow policies can model correlations between action dimensions, facilitating discovery of coordinated behaviors
  • Support finetuning of pretrained policies: Policies pretrained via behavior cloning can be improved through reward-based RL
  • Train policies from scratch: Learn complex control policies directly from environment rewards without requiring demonstrations

Limitations of Vanilla FPO

The Flow Policy Optimization (FPO) algorithm was proposed to enable PPO-style training of flow policies by replacing likelihood ratios with a surrogate based on conditional flow matching (CFM) losses. While FPO succeeds on simpler synthetic benchmarks like DeepMind Control Suite, we found it unstable for challenging real-world robotics tasks.

Challenges with Vanilla FPO in Robotics

  • High-dimensional action spaces: Humanoid robots have 19-29+ degrees of freedom, making optimization more challenging
  • Realistic physical constraints: Joint position limits, torque limits, and contact dynamics introduce discontinuities and hard constraints
  • Coarse reward signals: Robotics rewards are often sparse or shaped differently than game-like benchmarks
  • Gradient variance: The per-action ratio formulation in vanilla FPO leads to high gradient variance, causing unstable updates
  • Entropy collapse: Without proper regularization, flow policies can prematurely narrow their action distributions, losing the ability to explore and recover from local minima

These issues manifest as training instability: vanilla FPO policies are prone to catastrophic failures and local minima, even after extensive hyperparameter tuning. FPO++ addresses these challenges through per-sample ratio clipping and an asymmetric trust region that reduces gradient variance while preserving entropy.

Algorithm

We introduce FPO++, an improved flow policy gradient algorithm that enables stable training of flow-based policies for real-world robotics tasks. FPO++ builds upon the Flow Policy Optimization (FPO) framework, which bypasses intractable likelihood computation by using conditional flow matching (CFM) losses as a surrogate for the PPO likelihood ratio.

Background: Flow Matching Policy Gradients

Standard FPO replaces the PPO likelihood ratio \(\rho(\theta)\) with a likelihood-free surrogate based on CFM loss differences:

\[ \hat{r}_{\text{FPO}}(\theta) = \exp\left(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{CFM},\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t; o_t) - \hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{CFM},\theta}(a_t; o_t)\right) \]

where the CFM loss is estimated by sampling noise \(\epsilon_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\) and flow timesteps \(\tau_i \in [0, 1]\):

\[ \hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{CFM},\theta}(a_t; o_t) = \frac{1}{N_{\text{mc}}} \sum_{i}^{N_{\text{mc}}} \|\hat{v}_\theta(a_t^{\tau_i}, \tau_i; o_t) - (a_t - \epsilon_i)\|_2^2 \]

FPO++ Key Improvements

While vanilla FPO succeeds in synthetic benchmarks, we found it unstable for challenging robotics tasks. FPO++ introduces two key modifications:

1. Per-Sample Ratio

Unlike standard FPO which computes a single ratio per action after averaging across Monte Carlo samples, FPO++ computes ratios on a per-sample basis. For each sample \(i\), we calculate:

\[ \hat{\rho}_{\text{FPO}}^{(i)}(\theta) = \exp\left(\ell_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t, \tau_i, \epsilon_i; o_t) - \ell_\theta(a_t, \tau_i, \epsilon_i; o_t)\right) \]

This provides a finer-grained trust region by allowing each \((\tau_i, \epsilon_i)\) pair to be clipped independently, effectively increasing the batch size and reducing gradient variance.

2. Asymmetric Trust Region (ASPO)

FPO++ adopts an asymmetric trust region that treats positive and negative advantages differently:

  • Positive advantages (\(\hat{A}_t \geq 0\)): Use standard PPO clipping to decrease CFM losses
  • Negative advantages (\(\hat{A}_t < 0\)): Use the Simple Policy Optimization (SPO) objective, which provides a gradient signal that pulls ratios back instead of zeroing out gradients
\[ \psi_{\text{ASPO}}(\rho, \hat{A}_t) = \begin{cases} \min\left(\rho\hat{A}_t, \text{clip}(\rho, 1-\epsilon^{\text{clip}}, 1+\epsilon^{\text{clip}})\hat{A}_t\right), & \hat{A}_t \geq 0 \\[8pt] \rho\hat{A}_t - \frac{|\hat{A}_t|}{2\epsilon^{\text{clip}}}(\rho - 1)^2, & \hat{A}_t < 0 \end{cases} \]

ASPO actively disincentivizes large CFM loss increases, which (i) preserves entropy by penalizing aggressive decreases in action likelihoods, and (ii) stabilizes training by controlling the KL divergence between approximate and true posteriors.

Complete FPO++ Objective

Combining the per-sample ratio with the ASPO trust region, the full FPO++ objective is:

\[ \max_\theta \mathbb{E}_{a_t \sim \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t|o_t)} \left[ \frac{1}{N_{\text{mc}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{mc}}} \psi_{\text{ASPO}}\left(\hat{\rho}_{\text{FPO}}^{(i)}(\theta), \hat{A}_t\right) \right] \]

Zero-Sampling for Deployment

During training, FPO++ policies explore by sampling initial noise from \(\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\) and integrating over the learned flow field. At test-time, we initialize flow integration from \(\epsilon = \vec{0}\) (zero-sampling), which improves performance and enables reduced integration steps for lower-latency deployment on real robots.
